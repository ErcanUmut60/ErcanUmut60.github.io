Unsteady Adjoint Optimization of a Flame Deflector

M.Sc. Thesis · 2024 – 2026 · METU

When a solid rocket booster ignites, it launches strong, transient Ignition Overpressure (IOP) waves into the launch environment. These waves reflect off the flame deflector and travel back toward the vehicle, where the high-amplitude loads can damage the structure and sensitive payloads. My thesis asks whether the deflector itself can be reshaped to weaken these reflected waves.

Because IOP and the starting vortex that follows it are inherently unsteady, steady-state optimization is not enough. I used the unsteady discrete adjoint framework in the open-source SU2 solver to compute time-accurate shape sensitivities of a 45° flat-plate deflector, and modified the window-averaging formulation in the source code to support Lp-norm objectives that target peak pressures in time.

Two objectives were studied. Minimizing the pressure at a single probe point near the rocket reduced the objective by up to 12.3%, though convergence was oscillatory. Minimizing the surface- and time-averaged pressure on the rocket wall converged smoothly and reduced the objective by 32.6% with 25 design variables, and an L20 norm formulation further cut the peak pressure while producing a more buildable geometry. The entire workflow uses open-source tools, so the results can be reproduced.

Flame Deflector Investigation

TUBITAK 1001-ARDEB #123M355 · 2024 – 2025

2D Investigation

Before optimizing anything, the launch environment had to be simulated reliably. Using a benchmark configuration from the literature, a booster with a 0.1 m nozzle placed 0.5 m above a 45° flat-plate deflector, I ran time-accurate SU2 simulations with a chamber pressure ramped up to 782 kPa to mimic ignition. A verification study set the time step, inner-iteration tolerances and mesh resolution, and compared structured and unstructured grids.

Inviscid (Euler) and viscous (RANS) solutions were compared against probe-point data from earlier studies. Both captured the primary IOP waves with only minor differences, which showed that the far cheaper Euler simulations are sufficient for the optimization runs.

3D Investigation

To move toward realistic launch pads, I modeled a 3D launch pad geometry from the literature: a four-nozzle vehicle above a deflector with a 60° impingement section, a launch stand close to the rocket, and side walls that keep the flow within the pad. The fully structured volume mesh has nearly 15 million cells, and convergence was accelerated with multigrid on top of an adaptive CFL strategy.

Pressure probes at the nozzle centerline, the base of the rocket, the deflector surface and beneath the launch stand trace how the IOP waves spread through the pad over time. A single inviscid run takes more than a week and its contour output for just 0.03 s of flow reaches about 2 TB, which is why 3D unsteady adjoint optimization is planned as future work.
